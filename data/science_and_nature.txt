1. SPACE AND THE UNIVERSE

Space is the vast region beyond Earth’s atmosphere. It is mostly empty but contains stars, planets, moons, asteroids, comets, and galaxies. The universe is everything that exists, including all space, time, matter, and energy. Scientists believe the universe began with the Big Bang and has been expanding ever since.

Stars are huge balls of hot gas that produce light and heat through nuclear reactions. Our Sun is a medium-sized star. Galaxies are enormous collections of stars, gas, and dust held together by gravity. The Milky Way is the galaxy that contains our solar system. There are billions of galaxies in the universe.

2. THE SOLAR SYSTEM

The solar system consists of the Sun and everything that orbits it. The Sun is at the center and provides light and heat. Eight planets orbit the Sun. In order from the Sun they are Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune.

Mercury is the smallest planet and closest to the Sun. Venus is similar in size to Earth but has a very thick, hot atmosphere. Earth is the only planet known to have liquid water and life. Mars is called the Red Planet because of its rusty surface and has the largest volcano in the solar system.

Jupiter is the largest planet and is a gas giant with a Great Red Spot, which is a giant storm. Saturn is famous for its beautiful rings made of ice and rock. Uranus and Neptune are ice giants farther from the Sun and appear blue because of methane in their atmospheres.

Many planets have moons. Earth has one moon. Jupiter and Saturn have dozens of moons. The solar system also contains asteroids, mostly found between Mars and Jupiter, and comets, which are icy bodies that develop tails when they approach the Sun.

3. ANIMALS

Animals are living organisms that cannot make their own food and must eat plants or other animals. They are divided into two large groups. Vertebrates have a backbone. Invertebrates do not.

Vertebrates include fish, amphibians, reptiles, birds, and mammals. Fish live in water and breathe with gills. Amphibians such as frogs can live both in water and on land. Reptiles such as snakes and lizards have dry scaly skin. Birds have feathers and most can fly. Mammals have hair or fur and feed their young with milk. Humans are mammals.

Invertebrates include insects, spiders, worms, snails, and many sea creatures such as jellyfish and octopuses. Insects are the largest group of animals and have six legs. Spiders have eight legs.

Animals adapt to their environments. Camels store fat in their humps to survive in deserts. Polar bears have thick fur and fat to stay warm in the Arctic. Fish have streamlined bodies for swimming. Birds have lightweight bones and wings for flying.

Animals play important roles in nature. They help pollinate plants, spread seeds, control insect populations, and form part of food chains. Protecting animal habitats is important for keeping ecosystems healthy.

4. THE HUMAN BODY

The human body is made of many systems that work together.

The skeletal system provides structure and protection. Adults have two hundred and six bones. The skull protects the brain. The rib cage protects the heart and lungs. Bones are connected by joints that allow movement.

The muscular system works with the skeleton to produce movement. Muscles contract and relax. The heart is a special muscle that pumps blood.

The circulatory system moves blood through the body. The heart pumps blood through blood vessels. Blood carries oxygen, nutrients, and waste. Red blood cells carry oxygen. White blood cells help fight infection.

The respiratory system brings oxygen into the body and removes carbon dioxide. Air enters through the nose or mouth, goes down the windpipe, and reaches the lungs. In the lungs, oxygen enters the blood and carbon dioxide leaves it.

The digestive system breaks down food so the body can use it. Food travels from the mouth through the esophagus to the stomach and then the intestines. Nutrients are absorbed into the blood. Waste leaves the body.

The nervous system controls the body. The brain is the control center. The spinal cord carries messages between the brain and the rest of the body. Nerves carry signals that allow us to feel, move, and think.

The body also has a system for fighting disease called the immune system. Skin acts as a barrier. Special cells and chemicals attack germs that enter the body.

Humans need food, water, oxygen, and sleep to stay healthy. Exercise strengthens muscles and the heart. A balanced diet provides energy and materials for growth and repair.
